Mechanics

The numbers behind hunger, bonds, breeding, and money — for anyone who wants to know exactly why a fish did what it did.

Hunger

Hunger climbs on a deliberately slow clock, separate from the per-frame movement update — full to starving takes about 5 real minutes. A fish that goes long enough without reaching food in the main tank starts losing health from starvation instead, and can genuinely die this way. The one exception is a fish away exploring the Forest: it's somewhere you can't see or feed it, so hunger there simply caps out at "Low energy" instead of ever risking death — it gets a "Welcome back, looks a little hungry" nudge the moment it returns, not a punishment for having gone at all.

Feeding & treats

Fish Food is bulk and impersonal — buy a pack, drop it in the water, and whichever fish reaches it first eats it. Treats work differently: bought one pack at a time from the Shop, then fed directly to one chosen fish from its own Inspector.

TreatPricePack size
🦐 Brine Shrimp$105
🪱 Worms$85
🩸 Bloodworms$125
🦠 Plankton$95
🍕 Pizza$121

A treat relieves hunger by exactly the same amount as regular Fish Food — there's no bigger number for buying one, on purpose. What changes is the reaction: a species with a declared favorite (today, just the Axolotl — Brine Shrimp, Bloodworms, and Worms) lights up when fed one, shown as a nicer toast. Pizza sits outside that system entirely — nobody has it as a declared favorite, yet every fish is delighted by it regardless, which is exactly why it's kept to a single serving per purchase instead of a pack of five like the others.

Relationships

Every pair of fish shares one continuous bond score from −100 to 100, nudged by real events — waking each other, sleeping side by side, giving up a bed, a Friend nearby credited with a rescue when a Shark gets close — and slowly decaying if left alone. Forced proximity can hurt as easily as it helps: two fish who already dislike each other or worse, sharing a home overnight anyway, are pushed further apart instead of closer. The score maps to a state:

ScoreState
≤ −50😠 Rival
−49 to −15😒 Dislikes
−14 to 14Neutral
15 to 49🙂 Friend
≥ 50❤️ Best Friend

Each pair remembers up to 5 specific reasons for their score, oldest dropped first once that fills up. A Playful fish gets an extra boost to the score when it successfully wakes a friend up.

Sleep & wake-ups

About 1 in 5 fish (20%) are born Sleepy, independent of their personality. Everyone else wakes together the instant Night ends — a Sleepy fish genuinely doesn't: it stays asleep past the normal transition until a real wake attempt succeeds, resisting each one roughly 7 times out of 10. It's never permanently stuck, though — only a same-container Friend or Neutral tankmate ever bothers trying (a Rival won't), and each one is given a randomized number of chances before success becomes guaranteed: 3–5 for a Friend, 4–6 for a Neutral acquaintance. With nobody eligible in the room at all, a fallback timer wakes it anyway after about a minute.

A woken fish doesn't vanish the instant it wakes, either — it lingers a few seconds, and the whole room leaves together once every occupant is awake, not one at a time. See Decorations for the "Enter" view this all plays out in.

Breeding

Once a day, every mutual, grown-up, non-predator Friend or Best Friend pair has a 25% chance to have a baby, up to a cap of 30 total fish in the tank so it can't run away forever. A baby permanently remembers both of its parents from the moment it's born — see Dreams below for what that means the first time it has a nightmare.

Growth & Aging

Every fish visibly ages through four stages — Baby, Juvenile, Adult, Elder — a Baby doesn't even get its real sprite yet, so growing up is something you actually see rather than a number in the Inspector. Elder is worth a little less to sell than Adult, but not worthless, and once a day there's a small (4%) chance an Elder fish passes peacefully in old age — rare enough to notice, never routine.

Economy

The tank's total attractiveness comes from its fish and decorations: +5 per fish (+15 instead for a "rare" species — anything priced at $50 or more, which today just means the Betta and the Shark), plus a flat +10 if there's no food currently sitting uneaten. Visitor count is attractiveness ÷ 20; each visitor's $2 ticket is settled once a day along with a flat, unconditional $10/day "Aquarium Maintenance Grant." Donations ($1–3 per visitor) don't wait for that — the moment a visitor actually donates, the money lands immediately and a toast announces it ("A visitor donated $2!"), rather than being bundled into the day-end total.

Selling a decoration refunds half its purchase price.

If the tank goes fully bankrupt — $0, no food, no fish — Emergency Aquarium Welfare (on by default, toggleable in Settings) grants a fresh start: +1 fish, +25 food, +$20.

The Shop's own stock rotates once a day too — one species goes "Out of Stock" until the next day, so it's worth checking back on rather than a fixed catalog.

Happiness

A personality amplifier, not another stat to babysit: every fish sits in one of four Feeling bands driven by real events, never a hidden meter to grind for its own sake.

HappinessFeeling
< 30😞 Sad
30–54🙂 Neutral
55–79😊 Happy
≥ 80😄 Very Happy

A good night's sleep, a favorite treat, time with a friend, and a comfortable tank all push happiness up; a scare, a bad dream, a lost tankmate, or going hungry pull it down. It also decays gently toward 50 (comfortably inside Neutral) on its own, so a tank never just sits maxed out at Very Happy forever — real events are what actually gets it back up there, not time alone. At Very Happy, a fish gets small flourishes of its own — a sparkle, an excited wiggle, swimming in a happy circle, tailing a friend around — each on its own cooldown so they read as noticing a good mood, not background noise, and never while something genuinely serious just happened (a Shark, a death, a scare).

Relaxing

Any fish has roughly a 40% chance, checked periodically, of settling in at its favorite spot for a while — a quiet 😌 above it, nothing to manage. The Axolotl relaxes far more often (75%) and for longer, true to its resting-more nature. A friend passing by while one is already relaxing may stop and join it there instead of swimming on, its own small, always-noticed moment.

Exploration: The Forest

A one-time $700 unlock (Shop) opens a whole second place a hungry fish can travel to. Once past its own hunger threshold, a fish has roughly a 5% chance per check of setting off (boosted for Greedy and Explorer personalities, and a Friendly fish may tag along with a Friend already heading out regardless of its own hunger). Once there, it forages for wood, then carries a found piece home to sell for $15.

A rare Tiger Shark (about a 1% chance per check while anyone's present) can prowl in and startle every forager home empty-handed, dropping any wood they were carrying — nobody's ever actually hurt, just sent packing. It rests for a minute and a half after each visit before it's eligible to prowl in again, so a busy Forest can't get scared back-to-back.

Lost Adventure

Separately from an ordinary foraging trip, a healthy, well-fed fish (health 80+, hunger above the warning line) has a small 2%-per-day chance of getting properly lost in the Forest for several days (4–8) instead of just popping over and back. While lost, each day rolls one event: sheltering for the night in the Tree House, Hidden Cave, or Dense Plants Thicket (each a real, capacity-limited spot — briefly visible there, the same appear-and-vanish beat as a dream), a chance encounter with Bubbles (below), a close call with the Tiger Shark (a happiness dip, nothing worse), or a quiet day of just wandering and foraging wood. Coming home safely is a real, noticed moment — a happiness boost and a relationship bump with whoever missed it most.

Bubbles is a permanent resident of the Forest, not a random encounter — he's always relaxing in the Dense Plants Thicket by day. A lost fish that's gotten hungry enough (hunger under 60) will deterministically seek him out: with Driftwood in hand, he trades it for whichever food fits best (favorite food first, then a friend's favorite, from his stock of Brine Shrimp, Worms, and Plankton) — no Driftwood yet, and the fish is sent back to go find some first. Once night falls he heads out through the Hidden Cave, visible there just long enough to notice before he's gone — where he actually goes stays deliberately unexplained for now. He's back in the Thicket again every morning.

Day & night

Water temperature and light drift together on one continuous curve, warmest at noon and coldest at midnight. Fish move measurably slower once the water turns cold, and non-hungry fish stop swimming and sleep once night falls — a hard stop, not just a slowdown.

Achievements

Eleven account-wide milestones, from a first friendship to setting up Cloud Saves — tracked separately from any one save file, so a New Aquarium or a Load never resets what's already been earned. The Achievements menu is deliberately transparent: every name and description is visible whether it's unlocked or not, so it doubles as a light to-do list.

Dreams

About a quarter of tonight's actually-sleeping fish dream — shown as a 💭 next to their 😴, clickable into a small looping animated scene with a plain-language caption underneath. Which kind of dream leans on personality (Explorer → Fantasy, Greedy → Food, Shy → Home, Friendly → Friendship, Lazy/Playful → Happy) about 60% of the time, spread across the other categories the rest of the time — never an exclusive lock.

A fish's own Memory Log shapes it further: a recent shark scare gives a much-better-than-usual chance of a nightmare about it specifically; a recent memory naming its current Friend nudges toward a dream about them regardless of personality; and a tankmate it's lost can resurface in a dream of their own, for as long as that memory survives the log's cap — grief fading naturally as newer memories crowd it out, with no separate timer.

A nightmare is the one dream with real consequences. Five seconds in, the fish wakes up scared (😨, for a few seconds) right where it was sleeping. It then goes looking for comfort — a living parent first, if it has one, regardless of how close that relationship actually is (blood always outweighs an ordinary friendship score); only once no parent is left does it fall back to its Friend. Whichever it finds, it quietly relocates to sleep beside them — joining their claimed home if there's room, or just settling close on the floor if not — with a brief 🥺 once it arrives, and a diary line for both of them. With neither a parent nor a Friend, it simply settles back to sleep on its own.

Fish Memory Log

Every fish keeps its own diary, shown in its Inspector — up to 10 entries, oldest dropped first. Unlike a relationship's shared memories (only ever "why" for one specific pair), this is one fish's own history: a favorite treat, waking a friend up, becoming friends or rivals with someone, surviving a storm, escaping — or being saved from — a Shark, and a tankmate that isn't around anymore.
